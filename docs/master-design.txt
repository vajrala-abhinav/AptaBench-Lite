The one-sentence contract. Within each correctly linked paper, AptaBench Lite uses unique strict sequence anchors, sequence-associated name evidence, exhaustive full-gold semi-global comparisons, context-aware one-to-one assignment, conditional global-alignment tie-breakers and conservative abstention to establish associations; then it scores the same fixed associations under transparent strict/tolerant and whole/core definitions, permits visual human adjudication, and exports exactly the same result to the dashboard, Excel and JSON.

1. Status, scope, scientific philosophy and frozen decisions
1.1 Why AptaBench Lite exists
The rushed AptaBench deadline evaluator implements genuine sequence alignment, candidate generation, one-to-one assignment, review handling and reporting, but much of it is concentrated in large scripts and imports components from the original AptaBench V1.2 codebase. AptaBench Lite must be a small standalone application, not a cosmetic dashboard on the existing dependency chain. A fresh clone with its own documented installation must run without the old aptabench package, sibling virtual environments, external research-script paths, or Codex. The existing implementation is a behavioral reference for regression tests, not a runtime dependency.
The intended use is internal review of extractor .xlsx files by the research team and PI. It is not a universal aptamer identity oracle, a sequence generation system, or a mechanism for validating publications independently of gold curation. Its first job is to measure recovery against the team’s significant reference sequences and transparently expose uncertainty.

1.3 Three independent questions
The design always separates: (i) nucleotide comparison — how close are two strings under a defined alignment? (ii) record correspondence — does this extractor item represent this gold aptamer? (iii) reporting correctness — given a defensible correspondence, does its sequence pass the chosen strict/tolerant criterion? No function answering one question may secretly answer the other two. An excellent edit distance cannot establish identity when multiple golds compete; a trustworthy name can establish a plausible identity even when the nucleotides are wrong; a human association confirmation is not a manual TP override.
1.4 What this specification freezes, and what it does not
This document freezes observable behavior, invariants, input/output semantics, decision boundaries, user interactions and tests. It does not claim a statistically calibrated probability of identity, a biological minimum sequence length, or an experimentally validated optimum assignment margin. Where such a quantitative rule has not been agreed, implement a conservative manual-review fallback, log the exact reason, and do not invent a new confidence percentage. Development teams may select equivalent libraries and internal algorithms if tests and behavior remain identical.
1.5 Scope of other fields
The Lite dashboard can later extend to pool type/template and Kd, and the upload page may offer field selection. Those are future or separately specified modules. This document is normative for sequence matching and scoring. Pool or Kd evidence may be displayed as provenance/context only; neither can silently convert a sequence into a TP. The promised minimum complete application is the sequence module end to end.

2.2 Always-true invariants
A gold is never paired to an extractor from a different paper; paper alias collisions are errors/review, not invitations to sequence-search across papers.
One logical extractor item is assigned to no more than one gold; one gold is assigned to no more than one extractor. Legitimate duplicate-item capacity must remain explicit.
No gold record is removed from scoring because it was difficult to match, pending review or missing. Only documented scope/invalidity/attempt filters may remove gold from a declared denominator.
No raw extractor record is lost. Strict duplicate suppression is a scoring/search policy, not destructive deletion from the source/audit.
Changing a display scoring toggle does not change accepted associations, review decisions, raw items, or caches of alignments.
An out-of-tolerance human-confirmed correspondence remains a failed sequence value in a criterion that rejects it.
A manual-review record cannot become TP before a sufficiently resolved association exists; dashboard denominators never shrink merely due to abstention.
An exact semi-global core fit with long extractor flanks stays valid for core scoring even if whole-string completion is poor.
Nothing short of a one-to-one conflict-aware assignment can resolve contested candidate use; no Excel source order as a biological tie-breaker.
One stored alignment trace is the source for numerical edit counts, green/yellow/red highlighting and Excel presentation.
All automatic decisions have auditable reason codes; all human decisions are recorded and reversible with history.
E_p >= G_p signals potential numerical capacity, never confirmed sequence completeness.
3. End-to-end architecture and concept map

Figure 1. Normative algorithm-to-dashboard concept map. Arrows indicate data flow, not multiple independent evaluators.
3.1 Architectural layers
Input adapters read workbooks, select sheets, expose column maps, parse serialized list cells and assign source provenance. Normalization and linking safely resolve paper aliases, canonicalize parseable sequences and group exact duplicates. Candidate engine computes strict equality, name support, gold-near-duplicate warnings and full-gold fitting results. Resolver performs unique strict anchoring, one-to-one optimization, conditional global tie-breaking, competitor checks and abstention. Scorer receives the fixed effective association set and calculates the two population views under the four value criteria. Review service overlays human events and reruns only the necessary association/metric aggregation logic. Presentation adapters render Streamlit pages, colored alignments, Excel and JSON from the exact same result objects.
3.2 Non-circular execution rule
No scoring percentage may be used to select a more favorable association for the currently viewed metric. In particular, selecting Strict whole must not trigger a fresh assignment optimizing strict-whole TP count; selecting Adaptive tolerant + Core must not trigger a tolerant-specific reassignment. Association is resolved once using the prescribed evidence hierarchy. Every view scores that same fixed selection. A change to scientific matching policy or input workbooks is a new evaluation run with a new run ID; changing a display criterion is not.
3.3 Two kinds of state
Run state is immutable: input hashes, canonical objects, candidate comparisons, strict anchors, automatic association graph and automatic decisions. Review state is append-only and mutable only through validated events: confirmations, rejections, mark missing, reassignment, undo. The effective state is computed by replaying review events over the automatic run. The UI does not mutate its own data frame and assume the backend somehow agrees; the backend transaction validates and returns the effective result.
4. Source XLSX files, sheet/column mapping and ingestion
4.1 Supported upload contract
The minimum run requires (a) a gold .xlsx reference (uploaded or selected as the approved local team reference) and (b) an extractor .xlsx file. A user may choose an extractor worksheet and map its paper identifier, sequence values and optional name-evidence columns. A gold sheet and the relevant gold columns are also selectable. Read-only previews display mapped source columns and representative parsed cells before Run. A changed mapping invalidates the applicable run; it must not reinterpret old cached results silently.
Use the AptaBench legacy workbook format as an auto-detectable preset, not a hidden hardcoded requirement. Accept renamed column headers via manual mapping. Validate actual selected headers and value shapes; never assume every workbook will use the same physical letters. Workbook files must not be modified in place.

4.4 Parsing structured cells safely
Prefer strict JSON when possible. If historic input uses Python-style literal list/dict serialization, support a restricted safe parser such as ast.literal_eval after input-size and nesting limits; never call eval. Validate list/dict types and the expected sequence key. Preserve original notes; reject pathological nesting, unexpected binary values, excessive sequence lengths and unexplained multi-value encodings with actionable diagnostics. Map item-level names only if they are explicitly attached to the corresponding sequence item, never by coincidence of list order across independent worksheets.
4.5 Input preview and validation rules
Before Run, display selected sheet names, field mappings, row counts, paper count, count of significant gold records, count of scoreable gold bases and per-paper extractor item counts. Show invalid rows with reason, source cell and a fix suggestion. Distinguish an empty spreadsheet, absent sheet, malformed value list, empty extracted sequence, unrecognized status marker, duplicate paper ID alias and unsupported chemical sequence syntax. Invalid source data remains recorded but cannot be auto-declared a TP or silently discarded. A successful run must produce an input-validation report even when some records are excluded.
5. Paper linkage, attempted papers and significant-gold scope
5.1 Paper linking procedure
Index gold papers by stable paper_id, raw pdf_name and a conservative deterministic normalized filename alias. Preserve original strings.
Index each extractor pk under the selected paper-key column. Normalize only documented harmless filename differences, e.g., peripheral whitespace, terminal .pdf and known artifact suffixes if configured.
Use exact raw key agreement first; otherwise accept a unique documented normalized alias. Do not fuzzy-link by title or by nucleotide content.
If one extractor key maps to multiple gold papers, stop that paper with PAPER_LINK_AMBIGUOUS; do not compare candidate sequences across either paper.
If extractor key maps to none, retain its predictions as unmatched-paper inventory and surface a linkage issue. Do not silently count them against an unrelated reference paper.
Emit one PaperContext per linked attempted paper with its source keys, mapping evidence and immutable paper-scoped IDs.
5.2 Attempted-run population
A gold paper is attempted when the input batch explicitly contains its paper key or an independently configured batch manifest says it was processed. A present paper row with an empty sequence list is still an attempted paper: its scoreable significant golds can become FNs. A gold paper absent from the batch and manifest is unattempted and excluded from default per-run precision/recall denominators; report it separately. An unlinked extractor paper stays in the validation/linkage inventory until its paper association is corrected.
5.3 Significant reference selection
Within attempted, linked papers, select only records whose curated sequence annotation maps explicitly to significant. Non-significant records are excluded from every sequence candidate graph, strict lock, gold-near-duplicate check, association resolver and sequence denominator. Their rows remain in the underlying reference file, but Lite never uses them to improve a significant-only assignment. Do not use non-significant references to clear out otherwise unmatched extractor predictions; doing so would introduce an inconsistent comparison scope.
If a significant gold has a missing/unsupported canonical sequence, report a significant-but-unscoreable reference count. Exclude it only from numerical scoreable-gold denominator, not from the metadata/curation problem report. Never claim 100% significant-gold recovery without disclosing unscoreable significant golds. Maintain both G_significant_all and G_significant_scoreable.
5.4 Completeness count
For each paper, report G_p scoreable significant gold count, E_raw extractor item count and E_unique post-strict-dedup scoreable unique extractor prediction count. If E_unique >= G_p, label numerically sufficient candidates, not complete. If E_unique < G_p, label numerically insufficient to recover all golds one-to-one, unless a deliberately documented duplicate-multiplicity exception changes the logical item count. The completeness diagnostic must never force extra edges or modify the solver objective.
6. Sequence and name standardization contract
6.1 Standardization must be lossless and auditable
A normalized sequence is not a replacement for the raw cell. For every gold and extractor item, retain: raw_sequence, canonical_sequence when valid, parse_status, raw source location, exact list-item index, normalization operations in order, warnings, source-to-canonical character mapping (where possible), and rejected syntax. Display raw on request. A normalization transformation must not manufacture a nucleotide absent from the source. The engine never silently deletes internal letters or converts one biological alphabet into another.

6.3 Stable name keys and provenance
Names are processed independently from nucleotide strings. Preserve raw_name, name_source and name_confidence_class (e.g., sequence_item_note, gold_curated_label, paper_level_inventory_only). A strict name key may trim leading/trailing whitespace, collapse consecutive whitespace and apply Unicode case folding. Preserve significant digits, variant suffixes, hyphens, periods and other punctuation unless an explicitly approved synonym map exists. SMZ1 and SMZ1S are different; HF3-58 and HF3 58 are not a guaranteed identity.
An extractor-side paper-level aptamer_name list may have the same apparent length as the aptamer_seq list. This is not evidence of positional correspondence. Only names explicitly embedded in, or independently provenance-linked to, the specific sequence item are eligible to provide strong name evidence. Otherwise mark NAME_PAPER_LEVEL_ONLY and show the names as paper context with zero automatic identity weight.
6.4 Orientation and provenance of transformations
Forward sequence is the only automatically scored orientation in v0.1. An optional diagnostic may calculate reverse or reverse-complement distance, but the result must be tagged DIAGNOSTIC_REVERSE_OR_RC, visibly separate from forward and never used for a default TP. Likewise, no inferred fixed primer is appended to a gold core and no extractor flank is deleted during preprocessing. Only the fitting alignment determines which terminal bases are ignored for core matching.

7. Extractor deduplication and gold-near-duplicate diagnostics
7.1 Exact extractor deduplication, and nothing more
The duplicate equivalence key is (linked_paper_id, exact_canonical_forward_sequence). A group is formed only if both strings are validated and strictly equal after the approved standardization in §6. Never call alignment or tolerance to decide whether two predictions are duplicates. A one-base substitution, one-base insertion or deletion, reverse complement, or T/U change does not qualify as duplicate removal.
For each group, create duplicate_group_id, canonical_sequence, member extractor_item_ids, original positions/names/notes and one deterministic representative. Choose the representative by a stable source-ID sort, not extractor confidence or advantageous correctness. Compute the alignment once per unique canonical value; preserve individual members in the audit and detailed review. Default scoring uses unique canonical predictions (E_unique), matching the agreed Lite policy; label this convention in metadata. Raw item counts remain visible, so an analyst can recognize how much deduplication changed the denominator.
7.2 Suppression is not destruction
DUPLICATE_SUPPRESSED means excluded from the unique-prediction matching/scoring population, not erased from the source. The reviewer must be able to expand a duplicate group and see every original item and associated note. Exact duplicates with contradictory names must generate an identity warning; they may not silently inherit the representative’s name. A duplicate group is a sequence equivalence class, not automatically one aptamer identity.
7.3 Identical golds: the multiplicity exception
If multiple distinct significant gold records within one paper contain exactly the same canonical sequence, one sequence string alone cannot identify which gold record it corresponds to. The source still has distinct gold IDs and possibly distinct aptamer names. Strict whole equality does not justify arbitrarily locking one based on row order. Maintain enough extractor item capacity from an exact duplicate group to avoid deleting potentially legitimate independently named predictions, or flag the group as an explicit identity/multiplicity conflict for review. This exception must not reintroduce approximate deduplication: raw item presence and name evidence are the only reasons to retain distinct identical entries.
Two acceptable implementation patterns: (a) keep original items in the assignment graph for identity-conflict groups, while sharing the computed alignment; or (b) use a duplicate-group node with explicit capacity equal to retained raw members and reconstruct exact source IDs in assignments. The system must never assign one physical item ID twice.
7.4 Gold-near-duplicate diagnostic
Before complex resolution, within each paper compare the significant canonical gold records to each other. With at most roughly eight significant aptamers in the typical intended dataset, this is at most 8*7/2 = 28 pairs; the cost is negligible relative to full evaluation. Mark exact equality, edit distance, near-tolerance collisions, shared cores and potentially non-discriminative repeated motifs. One candidate is near-duplicate-sensitive when it plausibly matches two distinct golds under the configured tolerance. This diagnostic is evidence attached to the graph, not an independent deletion step and not an automatic verdict that either gold is wrong.
The baseline quantitative hint may mark a pair if its gold-to-gold edit distance is within max(B(L_i), B(L_j)); this is a diagnostic flag only, not a sufficient definition of identity or a replacement for the full-gold candidate comparison. A legitimate gold pair can differ by one edit and still represent different molecules. When the available evidence cannot distinguish them, abstain and show both.
7.5 Count invariants and audit
For each paper, keep E_raw, E_parseable_raw, E_unique, E_duplicate_suppressed, E_invalid, plus an explicitly documented multiplicity-exception count. They must reconcile to source item IDs. All unique source IDs used in accepted pairings must be traceable back to an original workbook row and item index. No post-hoc FP removal is permitted merely because a prediction was inconvenient to classify.
8. Alignment mathematics and error policy
8.1 Strict whole-string equality
After approved canonicalization, strict_whole(G,E) = (G == E). No edits, no overhangs, no reverse complement, no tolerated mismatch. This is our strongest nucleotide-and-scope agreement. It is not sufficient to distinguish two distinct gold records that happen to have identical strings, nor to overcome a material explicit name contradiction without surfacing review.
8.2 The primary semi-global/full-gold-fitting alignment
Use unit-cost Levenshtein alignment (match=0, substitution/insertion/deletion each 1) with all L gold nucleotides consumed. The extractor can have an arbitrary free terminal prefix and suffix, but no free internal skip: internal gaps count as indels. The best fit is the minimum edit distance between the entire gold and a contiguous region of the extractor; store the trace, fitted extractor start and end, and overhang lengths. Ordinary local alignment is not acceptable because it can ignore gold ends and inflate similarity.
If extractor is shorter, the same full-gold consumption requirement penalizes missing gold bases; it must not simply declare the shorter string a perfect partial-gold fit. For an extractor of length 40 against a gold of length 84, at least 44 gold bases must be represented as gaps/edits if it only contains a 40-base fragment. Reverse-complement hits can be diagnostic but not automatic passes.
A valid trace must include operation counts match, substitution, insertion, deletion, optional alignment gap positions, gold coordinate span [0,L) and extractor fitted interval. Store whether there are multiple equally minimal fitted intervals. For repeated motifs, ambiguous optimal placement is an identity warning even when the edit distance is zero.
8.3 Global alignment
Global alignment consumes both complete strings and penalizes every mismatch, inserted base, deleted base and terminal overhang. Compute it for whole-string value scoring, and conditionally for comparison of otherwise plausible competing semi-global candidate edges. Its completion is relative to gold length for comparability to the original formula; displayed percentages are clamped at zero. A long extractor containing an exact gold core can have terrible global completion but still be a legitimate core recovery. The global score has no authority to reject that unique core association by itself.
8.4 Frozen adaptive error budget
For positive gold length L, with integer arithmetic at the final decision:
Frozen budget formula: B(L) = max(1, min(4, floor(sqrt(L)/3))).
Implement the exact integer equivalent max(1, min(4, isqrt(L // 9))) if proved equivalent by tests for all supported lengths, or use an exact integer square-root strategy that cannot incur floating-point boundary drift. L=0 is invalid, not a zero-length TP. The original evaluator had a zero-edit allowance for golds shorter than 9 nt; Lite raises that minimum to one because a one-base deletion in an 8-nt aptamer can still represent a meaningful, recoverable extraction.

8.5 Completion and threshold decision
For edit distance d under the selected comparison mode, define completion_pct = 100 * max(0, 1 - d/L). The strict criterion is d == 0; the adaptive criterion is d <= B(L), using integer d and B(L), never rounded display percentages. A displayed 96.4% cannot override a distance failure. Optional diagnostic 2*B(L) is allowed in internal technical exports but is not a main dashboard toggle and is not used to change matching associations.

9. Strict locking, name evidence and candidate-graph construction
9.1 Ordered matching pipeline and each stage’s hand-off
Stage S0 — Preflight. Receive valid linked paper context with significant scoreable golds, parseable extractor candidates, IDs, duplicate groups and gold-similarity warnings. Does: compute scope and diagnostics. Does not: mark any TP, make fuzzy paper links or drop the hard cases. Handoff: a PaperContext with all unassigned nodes.
Stage S1 — Strict whole-string anchors. Compare normalized forward strings for exact equality. Lock a candidate only if the gold and extractor are uniquely paired under the defined raw/duplicate multiplicity rule and there is no material unresolved identity contradiction. Mark both as MATCHED_STRICT, store reason UNIQUE_STRICT_WHOLE, and remove these item IDs from subsequent assignment. Does not: tolerate edits, guess a name, resolve identical-gold collisions by row order or silently consume one extractor claimed by several strict-identical golds. Handoff: immutable strict anchors plus remaining gold and extractor IDs. If a material name conflict is discovered later, preserve the strict sequence evidence but reopen review status; never hide the conflict simply to keep metrics high.
Stage S2 — Name evidence. For each remaining gold/extractor pair with a credible sequence-local exact name agreement, call the same alignment engine on that pair (a restricted, early mini-pass). Record name match, source, strict/core distance and any contradiction. Does: prioritize or corroborate a candidate and expose a probable wrong-name extraction. Does not: lock on name alone or convert an out-of-budget nucleotide sequence to TP. Handoff: updated evidence annotations on still-unassigned nodes. Paper-level name arrays are not attached by item index.
Stage S3 — Exhaustive sequence comparisons. For every remaining significant gold compare against every remaining unique extractor item within that paper. Compute canonical full-gold semi-global edit distance and trace, tolerance, completion, overhangs, sequence lengths and alternative placements. Does: build all eligible edges and retain weak/failed comparisons as diagnostics. Does not: assign while looping, omit the runner-up, compare across papers or drop candidate edges just because they do not rank first. Handoff: complete per-paper candidate graph.
Stage S4 — Context and competition. Attach gold-near-duplicate flags, candidate overlaps, sequence-local name support/conflict, shared primer/low-complexity warnings, exact core flags and contested connected components. Does: determine which pairs are viable for automatic assignment and which require stronger evidence. Does not: create a new arbitrary confidence percentage. Handoff: annotated graph with possible dummy/unmatched alternatives.
Stage S5 — Global assignment. Solve compatible one-to-one associations for the entire remaining paper, preserving available unmatched nodes. Does: use all viable edges and the agreed evidence hierarchy to choose globally coherent candidate links. Does not: require E >= G to imply all golds matched, permit an extractor ID to be reused, or make row order a biological tie-breaker. Handoff: tentative assignments plus the unresolved contested components.
Stage S6 — Conditional global tie-break and runner-ups. Only when two or more viable semi-global candidates compete, compare full-string global edit distances/completion for the affected pairs. Evaluate their effect on the whole connected component, not just a local pair. If the distinct global preference is coherent and there is no material conflict, it can resolve the tie. Where still ambiguous, examine the affected candidates’ runner-ups using the already computed candidate graph. Does not: globally penalize every core match or keep rescoring forever. Handoff: stable preferred assignments or explicit unresolved reason codes.
Stage S7 — Final automatic acceptance gate. Verify unique IDs, evidence provenance, required tolerance for auto-links, absence of unresolved name contradictions, gold-duplicate ambiguity and unresolved equivalent graph solutions. Accept defensible pairs; otherwise mark relevant records MANUAL_REVIEW or UNMATCHED. Does not: force an answer or classify a failed nucleotide value as correct. Handoff: immutable AutomaticPaperResult plus a reproducible decision audit.
Stage S8 — Sequence scoring. After associations are fixed, compute four display correctness criteria and both population views. Does: derive metrics from G, E, accepted M and passing T. Does not: select new associations, delete unmatched predictions or turn review into TP. Handoff: dashboard data and exporters.
9.2 All-pairs means all pairs, even with strict locks elsewhere
For the subset of nodes remaining after unique strict anchors, construct all G_remaining × E_remaining comparisons; each candidate has a stable ID. It is acceptable to reuse cached edit distances for identical canonical strings, but the identity evidence belongs to the individual source item. The paper’s event log should still show strict-anchor provenance and any candidate rivals that were effectively preempted by a strict lock, so the reviewer can explain why a remaining gold’s candidate was unavailable.
9.3 What name evidence can and cannot do
An extractor stating that a particular sequence item is named DUX4, when gold DUX4 exists in the same paper, is a powerful hypothesis. The name-supported pair gets an early exact/semi-global comparison and a NAME_EXACT_SEQUENCE_LOCAL reason. If its nucleotides are within tolerance, it can improve the candidate’s support. If the same extractor’s nucleotide string is an exact whole match for a different gold called APT7, the system must preserve the contradiction (NAME_SEQUENCE_CONFLICT) rather than silently ‘fix’ the extractor’s claimed name. Name evidence can be mistaken; it is neither infallible ground truth nor irrelevant. A material contradiction sends the affected case to review if it prevents an unambiguous automatic interpretation.
9.4 Exact core vs strict whole
Exact fitted core (semi_global_d=0) provides excellent nucleotide correctness but does not have the same identity specificity as strict whole when multiple golds share central regions. It remains in the contextual graph. Only unique whole-string exact pairs are locked in S1. When an exact core fit is the only credible candidate and no conflicts exist, the engine may accept it in S7. When a full-length gold and core-only gold compete for the same longer extractor, global scope agreement can break the competition.
10. Global assignment, conditional tie-breakers and runner-ups
10.1 Represent the paper as a bipartite graph
Left-side nodes are significant gold record IDs; right-side nodes are eligible unique extractor IDs (or explicit multiplicity-capacity nodes in a gold-duplicate exception). Each candidate edge has primary alignment evidence, tolerance status, name support, global scope evidence where needed, and warning flags. Insert dummy unmatched alternatives. A matching is legal only if each physical ID occurs at most once. A suitable exact solver is a Hungarian algorithm or equivalent maximum-weight bipartite assignment; the old rushed evaluator already used a form of Hungarian assignment, so this is a semantic upgrade, not the first use of graph optimization in AptaBench.
10.2 Avoid a magic confidence score
The decision objective should follow a documented priority ordering: unique strict whole anchors are already removed; surviving edges must satisfy basic viability; explicit reliable names and uniquely discriminative full-gold fits support identity; whole-paper uniqueness and conditional global scope can distinguish remaining competitors; an unresolved material contradiction is a stop signal. A lexicographic objective (evidence classes and deterministic comparisons) is preferable to an invented mix such as 0.4*name + 0.3*completion + 0.3*length. A solver’s numeric weight can encode lexicographic categories internally, but this must never be shown as a calibrated probability of identity.
10.3 When global alignment runs
Only activate global alignment as an association tie-breaker for contested viable semi-global candidates (or when calculating the independently displayed whole-string scoring criterion). Compare their whole-string edit distances under unit edit costs. A one-edit improvement is a real discrete difference, not mere floating noise, but it is not automatically sufficient to negate a material independent identity conflict. If the preferred assignment is globally compatible, uniquely best under documented evidence priorities, and not contradicted, accept. If global completion does not reliably distinguish, stop and manual review; do not introduce arbitrary 2%/5% margins or an unlimited search to rescue a hard match.

10.5 Runner-ups, counterfactuals and the stopping rule
The historical first-best-versus-second-best greedy pass is removed: every candidate comparison is already in the graph. For a contested component, however, the engine should inspect the runner-up for a gold and the runner-up gold for a claimed extractor. It may solve the affected component with a proposed edge forbidden to test whether an equivalent alternative exists. This is a bounded stability diagnostic, not a perpetual counterfactual rescue cascade. If the next-best complete assignment is equivalent under the frozen priority policy, if competing golds are indistinguishable, or if global alignment cannot make a reliable decision, mark MANUAL_REVIEW and stop. No mandatory numerical margin is introduced in v0.1.
10.6 Outcomes when no viable unique association exists
If all candidates are outside standard tolerance, retain them for manual inspection; do not automatically call one a TP because it is numerically the best available. If a reliable sequence-local name exists but nucleotide distance exceeds budget, mark identity hypothesis + OUTSIDE_TOLERANCE_REVIEW, not an automatic pass. If no extractors are present in an attempted paper, all eligible golds are unassigned and count unrecovered in closed-world scoring. If an extractor remains after all golds have unambiguous accepted associations, it remains UNVERIFIED_EXTRA; do not force its use.

12. Sequence scoring definitions and metric populations
12.1 Scoring happens after association, never during a display toggle
A finished automatic run produces a canonical set A_auto of accepted one-to-one associations plus unresolved nodes. Human review may produce an effective set A_effective by applying valid events. The scorer receives one selected result population (automatic-only or effective adjudicated, clearly marked in the report) and does not run assignment. It computes the chosen criterion for each accepted association using previously stored distance/trace data. No criterion is allowed to cherry-pick the alternate extractor candidate that would improve its own F1.
The default PI dashboard uses the effective/current associations and labels them Automatic only or After human review as appropriate. Full audit must retain the pristine automatic metrics separately. Any correction to the reference gold base string is an explicit new reference-version evaluation, not a hidden manual score edit.

12.4 Score-count definitions
Within the declared attempted-and-linked significant-only scoreable population define:
G: count of scoreable significant gold records in attempted papers.
E: count of unique scoreable extractor prediction items after the frozen strict deduplication policy and documented multiplicity exceptions.
M: count of accepted one-to-one gold–extractor associations, automatic plus applicable confirmed human associations.
T: count of the M accepted associations whose value passes the currently selected strict/tolerant × whole/core criterion.
R: count of gold records still pending manual review; U: count of unmatched unique extractor predictions. These are reported as operational statuses, not added a second time to E or G.
Every accepted association uses one gold and one extractor ID, so 0 <= T <= M <= min(G,E) under ordinary distinct-item scoring. Multiplicity exceptions must still trace to source IDs and preserve these inequalities with the properly defined E.
12.5 All sequences / closed-world significant-reference metrics
When All sequences is selected, every scored extractor prediction not contributing a successful accepted recovery is counted provisionally as a false positive relative to the significant gold set. Every significant gold not successfully recovered is counted as FN. This intentionally penalizes extra outputs and manual-review non-successes; it does not prove that every extra aptamer sequence is biologically wrong. The user must see this exact caveat once in the UI.
All-sequences counts: TP = T; FP = E - T; FN = G - T.
All-sequences metrics: Precision = T / E; Recall = T / G; F1 = 2*T / (E + G).
Use N/A when a denominator is zero rather than declaring success. For example, no attempted predictions and G>0 gives recall 0, undefined precision, and F1 0 under the explicit zero-TP convention, with a note that precision is undefined. No attempted papers should produce no applicable run metrics, not a synthetic 100%. Unlinked papers and unscoreable reference rows are listed separately until the scope is resolved.
An accepted association whose sequence fails the displayed criterion contributes one FP for its extractor and one FN for its gold. This is deliberate: the incorrect prediction exists, and the correct significant reference has not been recovered. It does not add a second FP/FN just because the pair also has a review row.
12.6 Matched-only conditional correctness
When Matched only is selected, evaluate only the M accepted associations. Each association is a paired trial: T pass, M-T fail. Display Matched-pair accuracy/correctness = T/M, with T of M accepted pairs pass. If the UI also exports matched-only precision, recall and F1 under symmetric paired counting, all three are T/M because the compared gold and extractor populations have equal size M. They are not independent estimates of total extractor precision. A clearer dashboard emphasizes Matched-pair correctness, not three redundant KPIs.
Matched-only paired metrics: Precision = Recall = F1 = T / M when M > 0.
If M=0, show N/A and a clear 0 accepted pairs label; never display a fabricated 100%. Beside matched-pair correctness, show significant-gold recovery T/G and the raw accepted-pair count so a conservative evaluator cannot improve its headline by flagging all difficult golds for review. For an accepted pair outside tolerance, matched-only correctness fails under that criterion even if the association was manually confirmed.
12.7 All-sequences caveat text: frozen UI wording
All sequences (closed-world): This score checks every unique extractor prediction against the significant gold reference only. Extractors can report more sequences than the significant gold set contains, including legitimate sequences outside this reference. Those additional predictions lower closed-world precision and F1 by design; they are not independently verified biological false positives. Unresolved manual-review candidates count as unconfirmed recoveries until adjudicated.
This message should appear once near the controls when All sequences is selected and be included in the exported methodology/run metadata. Do not substitute the inaccurate blanket claim that scores are ‘always terrible’; they can be low when many extra predictions exist, but their value depends on the actual counts.
12.8 Manual review and provisional FP/FN
A pending gold is already included in G-T; a pending extractor is included in E-T. Label these contributions provisional closed-world non-successes. They are not separately counted on top of the formulas. After a reviewer confirms a correspondence, the pair becomes part of M; it increases T only when its actual aligned value passes the selected criterion. A reviewer-confirmed incorrect pair increases matched-only denominator M but not T. Rejecting a candidate or marking gold missing does not manufacture a TP. An explicit reviewer decision may change which extractor is linked to which gold, but must always maintain one-to-one IDs.
12.9 Worked example: five golds and eight predictions
Assume G=5 significant scoreable golds, E=8 unique predictions, M=4 accepted associations, and all four accepted pairs pass Adaptive tolerant + Core (T=4). Then All sequences: TP=4, FP=8-4=4, FN=5-4=1, precision 50%, recall 80%, F1 8/(8+5)=61.54%. Matched only: 4/4 = 100% conditional matched-pair correctness; significant-gold recovery is 4/5 = 80%. The fifth gold remains unrecovered; four predictions lack accepted successful links. Do not randomly remove three unmatched predictions to force E=G; that would artificially inflate precision from 50% to 80% and collapse precision/recall into a count-balance artifact.
Now suppose only three of those four accepted associations pass Strict whole (T=3), without changing a single association. All-sequences precision 37.5%, recall 60%, F1 6/13=46.15%; matched-pair correctness 3/4 = 75%; significant-gold recovery 3/5 = 60%. The differing scores reflect the selected nucleotide criterion, not different matching outcomes.
12.10 Worked example: manual review pending
For G=5, E=8, and only T=3 accepted passing pairs, with two golds and several extractors pending review: all-sequences TP=3, FP=5, FN=2, precision 37.5%, recall 60%, F1 46.15%. These are provisional because suspected associations are unresolved. If one pending gold/extractor pair is human-confirmed and passes the selected threshold, T becomes 4, FP becomes 4, FN becomes 1, and F1 becomes 61.54%. If the person confirms the association but its bases fail tolerance, M increases while T does not; closed-world F1 stays 46.15%, and matched-only correctness declines because a known incorrect extraction is now included.
12.11 Suggested reporting metadata
Every metric payload must include population_label, n_gold_scoreable, n_extractor_unique, n_accepted_pairs, n_passing_pairs, n_pending_gold, n_unverified_extractor, strict_or_tolerant, whole_or_core, review_mode (automatic/effective), exact numerator/denominator, and explanatory notes. A percentage without its evaluated population is an incomplete benchmark result. Disclose the distinct count of unscoreable significant gold records and unattempted gold papers adjacent to the summary when nonzero.
13. Manual review and post-review metrics
13.1 Pending association is not incorrect sequence
The engine flags MANUAL_REVIEW whenever it cannot defensibly identify a counterpart, when rival assignments remain equivalent, or when the source/identity evidence is contradictory. This is an association uncertainty, not a claim that the extracted nucleotides are wrong. It may contain an exact 100% core fit that is still ambiguous between golds. Consequently it contributes no TP until resolved but retains candidate matches, edit distances and sources for inspection.
A different case is confirmed identity but questionable nucleotides. This must remain in Matched only and be marked correct/incorrect according to the selected value criterion. Otherwise matched-only results would look artificially perfect by excluding all known wrong sequences.

14.4 Exact action semantics
Confirm association. User selects gold G and extractor E, clicks Confirm, and receives a preview with alignment and any warnings. Engine validates paper, item existence, uniqueness and duplicate constraints. If E is unassigned and G has no conflicting accepted link, append CONFIRM_ASSOCIATION(G,E) to review events; set effective MATCHED_HUMAN; calculate all four value pass flags using stored trace. The user cannot click TP; confirmation is identity only. If d > B(L) show a warning and still allow confirmation because the extractor can have wrong bases while its identity is known.
Reject candidate. Append REJECT_PAIR(G,E). Rejects only that edge, not the entire gold or extractor. The gold remains pending if unresolved; other candidates stay available. Do not infer a false negative solely from rejection of one proposed pair.
Mark gold missing. Reviewer explicitly states that no acceptable extracted counterpart for that significant gold exists in the selected batch. Append MARK_GOLD_MISSING(G) with optional note; effective status becomes MARKED_MISSING_HUMAN. The gold remains unrecovered/FN under closed-world metrics. This action must be reversible, and the UI should warn when unreviewed plausible candidates remain.
Leave unresolved. No new association; optionally append a reviewer note. Candidate remains MANUAL_REVIEW and paper retains pending status. No TP created; all-sequences counts remain conservative.
Reassign. If selected E is already linked to another gold G_old or selected G is already linked to E_old, show a conflict dialog listing the current assignment, proposed assignment, affected golds/extractors and metric consequences. Require explicit confirmation and a reason; append one atomic REASSIGN event that frees old endpoints and establishes the new pair. Never silently override a unique strict lock or a human-approved pair. Both affected statuses and metric aggregates must update together or not at all.
Undo. Undo should target a specific review action/revision, validate that the current state is compatible, and append a compensating UNDO event or an immutable superseding revision. The prior human decision remains in the audit trail. Never delete the original automatic result.
14.5 Availability and conflict policy
An extractor ID cannot be assigned to two golds. The right panel visibly labels Available, Suggested, Assigned to G#, Duplicate-suppressed or Invalid; assigned items remain viewable but selecting them for a new association invokes the explicit reassignment flow. A gold that has been marked missing cannot silently acquire an extractor; the reviewer must undo the missing decision or use a deliberate conflicting-state correction. Rejecting a pair must not automatically free an unrelated accepted match.
14.6 Post-action computation
After a review event, do not rerun the complete extractor parse or all pairwise DP alignments. Replay/commit the review transaction against the cached AutomaticRun, enforce one-to-one constraints, recalculate affected effective associations, rerender relevant candidate availability and recompute score aggregations from fixed alignment evidence. Suggested candidate ordering may refresh using existing graph data; it must not silently rematch previously accepted records. A new input file, schema mapping, canonicalization policy or matching policy version intentionally creates a new run and invalidates the relevant cache.
14.7 When the paper is considered finished
A paper is REVIEW_COMPLETE when every scoreable significant gold has an effective accepted association or an explicit reviewed-missing outcome, with no unresolved material identity contradiction. The paper can be complete with zero TP if every gold was reviewed missing or every linked sequence is incorrect. A paper with all golds matched but unresolved name/identity conflicts should remain flagged for inspection. An extractor-only extra does not by itself keep the paper in manual review; it stays in closed-world scoring.
14.8 Persistence and shared-team review
The app must save reviewer events in a durable, importable form. Local-only single-user MVP: an append-only review-state JSON file with atomic writes, checksum and explicit Save/Download/Import workflow; preferably autosave per committed event. Streamlit session state alone is not durable storage: browser reloads can reset it. If the app later becomes shared by multiple researchers, use authenticated users, per-run persistence (e.g., SQLite/PostgreSQL), optimistic version checks and transaction/locking to prevent simultaneous edits to the same paper from overwriting each other. Reviewer initials alone are insufficient for attribution on a publicly reachable multiuser server.
15. Dashboard information architecture and user experience
15.1 Four primary user screens
A. Upload & Configure. Upload extractor XLSX; select approved gold XLSX or upload custom reference; choose relevant sheets; map gold paper ID, source name, record ID, significant-status and sequence columns; map extractor paper key, sequence value and optional item-level name/context columns. Show data preview, validation errors and selected attempted-paper scope. Provide one Run evaluation action. Keep rarely used normalization/scientific options behind an Advanced expander, with immutable versioned defaults.
B. Overview. Show run banner (input names/versions, attempted papers, significant golds, accepted matches, pending review), the three simple scoring choices, compact KPI values and a per-paper results table sortable by pending count or recovery. All-sequences mode shows precision/recall/F1 with the caveat; Matched only shows Matched-pair correctness plus significant-gold recovery/denominator, not three redundant F1/P/R values. The app can display raw TP/FP/FN beneath KPIs and small n/N denominators without new toggles.
C. Paper Detail / Sequence. One row per scoreable significant gold; show accepted extractor or unresolved status, sequence names, lengths, matching stage, edit distance, tolerance, raw/normalized reference, score flags and aligned-color preview. Allow sorting/filtering by status, but no separate Review Queue tab. The review action lives in the paper sequence detail itself. A read-only alignment inspector is available for automatic matches.
D. Manual Review & Export. Within a paper, the side-by-side gold/extractor workspace supports exact actions in §14. The Export area offers current effective Excel, machine-readable JSON/audit and optional reviewer-state backup. Exports disclose automatic vs reviewed metrics and selected scoring criteria; no second evaluator is invoked.
15.2 Main screen interaction contract
Only three primary controls—All sequences/Matched only; Strict/Adaptive tolerant; Whole string/Core only—govern displayed sequence scoring. Switching one control must return instant results from precomputed per-pair distances and fixed IDs. Keep manual_review count, G, E, M, T and run mode visible enough that the PI understands what population is assessed. A record can display a green core alignment while retaining a yellow identity pending status; color is nucleotide evidence, not a promise of correct identity.
15.3 Paper cards and navigation
The Overview should list every attempted linked paper, with paper_id, paper name, significant gold count, accepted count, unrecovered count, pending review count, and closed-world or matched-only result under the currently selected criterion. Clicking a paper opens its Sequence detail; selecting a sequence reveals alignment and provenance. The Resolve manual review (N) action appears at the bottom of paper detail if needed. For an all-resolved paper the action is replaced by Evaluation complete, while optional Inspect/Correct remains accessible without making mandatory work.
15.4 App behavior on error, zero records and invalid inputs
A malformed source file should produce a specific error near its mapping/row preview; it should not crash to a blank page or return 100% metrics. If the extractor has zero valid sequences for a legitimately attempted paper, show significant gold count, zero accepted pairs, expected FNs, and an Extractor empty note. If all paper keys are unlinked, stop the benchmark summary and request linkage correction. If the significant gold set is empty, show No scoreable significant gold sequences rather than undefined 0/0 as 100%.
15.5 Accessibility and PI friendliness
Use descriptive labels rather than internal function names (Core matching, not semi_global_DP_v3). Keep scientific caveats one paragraph long, reveal reason codes only on demand, and never use color alone to convey correctness; show numeric edit counts and gap symbols. Preserve keyboard navigation as Streamlit allows, clear focus in review actions, adequate contrast, and a readable monospaced font for nucleotides. Protect scientific accuracy in a novice-friendly dashboard instead of hiding important denominators.

GoldRecord must have stable paper and record IDs, raw/canonical bases, curated label, significant status and source provenance. 17.4 Explicit matcher pseudo-code
def evaluate_paper(paper, policy):    assert paper.paper_link_is_unambiguous    golds = paper.scoreable_significant_golds    extractors = paper.unique_canonical_extractor_items    gold_flags = compare_gold_to_gold(golds, policy)    strict_anchors, leftovers = lock_unique_exact_whole_pairs(        golds, extractors, gold_flags)    name_evidence = collect_sequence_local_name_evidence(leftovers)    name_evidence = compare_named_pairs_with_alignment(name_evidence)    edges = []    for gold in leftovers.golds:        for extractor in leftovers.extractors:            edges.append(compare_full_gold_to_extractor(                gold, extractor, policy))    graph = build_paper_graph(edges, gold_flags, name_evidence,                              allow_unmatched=True)    proposals = solve_one_to_one(graph, strict_anchors, policy)    conflicts = identify_competing_components(graph, proposals)    proposals = apply_conditional_global_tiebreaks(        proposals, conflicts, graph, policy)    proposals = bounded_runner_up_check_if_needed(proposals, graph)    decisions = validate_or_abstain(proposals, graph, policy)    return build_automatic_paper_result(        paper, strict_anchors, graph, decisions)
This describes logical order, not a requirement to recompute the same distance multiple times. All calls must use a shared distance/trace provider. A reviewer confirmation does not call evaluate_paper again; it applies a validated overlay to its output.
17.5 A run configuration must be reproducible
The full config records source mapping, selected sheets and parser mode; significant-status allowed values; exact canonicalization policy; paper alias rules; attempted-paper declaration; duplicate suppression convention; error-budget version; eligible automatic-match policy; solver/tie-break version; and export display defaults. The three dashboard display toggles are not part of the association-policy hash. For each run, store Python/package versions and Git commit/tag when available. Changes to a scientific default require a new policy version and regression tests, not a silent refactor.
18. Caching, performance, determinism and invalidation
18.1 Why the old workflow felt slow
Running Codex repeatedly to execute scripts, inspect independently generated outputs, and recalculate workbook metrics is a development-audit workflow—not how a scientist should operate a finished evaluator. In Lite, the app calls the Python engine directly, gets a typed result, and uses it for views/export. Routine runs do not invoke an LLM, API key, model inference, PDF parsing, network service or GPU. The workbooks already contain extracted sequences; this tool is a post-extraction evaluator.
18.2 Compute only once per unique comparison
Exact equality uses a direct canonical string comparison before dynamic programming. Use exact duplicate-grouping to avoid repeated identical DP work; each distinct (gold canonical, extractor canonical, algorithm mode, policy version) is computed once and cached. Name-mini-alignment uses the same provider that the exhaustive candidate graph uses; no duplicate independent implementation. Reuse the semi-global trace for candidate evidence and the manual-review alignment preview. Compute global distances when needed for whole-string score views or contested tie-breaks; cache them separately. Score toggles use d_global, d_semiglobal and budgets already available in result objects.
18.3 Streamlit reruns are not evaluations
Streamlit re-executes app code on interactions; therefore separate (a) a button-driven, explicitly committed Run evaluation that establishes an immutable run ID, (b) cached deterministic parsed/evaluated data, (c) ephemeral selected paper/gold/extractor screen state, and (d) durable review-event storage. Use st.cache_data for serializable derived data and/or a domain-specific persistent alignment cache keyed by validated inputs. Use st.session_state for screen selection and temporary UI state, never as the sole durable review history. Prevent rendering a new graph or erasing a reviewer decision on harmless filter change.

Sorting input rows, shuffling extractor list order, or changing iteration order must not change evidence-based accepted correspondences. 19. Local installation, team access and hosted deployment
19.1 Recommended first distribution: local browser application
AptaBench Lite is a Python application with a browser interface. Running streamlit run app.py starts a Python server on the user’s computer; the browser displays its UI. The alignment and scoring are executed by Python on the server machine. No Codex account, AI token, internet connection for computation, dedicated graphics card or centralized database is required for the local single-user version once dependencies are installed. A localhost URL is not automatically accessible to other team members.
For the first PI/team handoff, prefer the complete source repository plus a simple Windows launcher/README or a preconfigured internal workstation. The source must be cloned/installed on each member’s computer for independently running local sessions, unless the lab deliberately deploys one authenticated shared server. GitHub Desktop makes cloning and updating the repository easier, but does not itself install Python libraries or host a live Streamlit instance.
19.2 System requirements and dependency classes
A compatible desktop operating system: Windows, macOS or Linux; current mainstream web browser; Python 3.11/3.12 recommended as a stable baseline subject to pinned package compatibility testing. Streamlit’s published support range can evolve; the project’s tested range is the final authority.
Python environment manager (venv) and package manager (pip); access to package downloads on initial installation unless dependencies are vendored under approved license.
Core dependencies, pinned only after implementation/testing: streamlit, an Excel reader such as openpyxl or equivalent, a writer supporting rich-text colored output such as xlsxwriter/appropriate engine, pandas if needed for tables, an assignment solver (scipy or a tested pure-Python implementation), a validated typed-model library if selected, and pytest for development. numpy is optional unless chosen by the implementation. No LLM SDK or AptaBench V1.2 package.
Writable location for optional review JSON snapshots/caches and exported workbooks. Browser-uploaded content can live temporarily in memory, but human review must be explicitly persisted.
Network access only if deploying or installing packages; a local run with dependencies present can work offline on local XLSX files.

20. Privacy, security, concurrency and recovery
20.1 Uploaded research data and retention
Local Streamlit still uses a Python server, but that server runs on the local computer; remote deployment sends uploaded workbook data to the remote server. Make this distinction explicit in the README and Privacy text. Never transmit sequences, names or papers to analytics/LLM APIs unless separately authorized. Avoid accidental logging of whole unpublished sequences or extracted XLSX cells to shared application logs. In hosted deployments, define the retention policy for uploaded bytes, temporary files, cached comparisons, exports and reviewer history; implement deletions consistent with lab requirements.
20.2 Input-hardening and provenance
Limit file types, upload size, extracted cell/list length and nesting depth. Treat everything from XLSX as untrusted input: no eval, no arbitrary code execution, no unsafe pickle imports, no HTML/JavaScript injection via notes, no arbitrary filesystem paths derived from filenames. On Excel export, defend against spreadsheet formula injection when a raw user-supplied value starts with characters that Excel may execute as formulas. Handle exceptions without exposing raw filesystem secrets, and preserve full original source text safely for audited reviewers.
20.3 Review integrity
Each review transaction must verify current run fingerprint, source IDs and current review revision. Cross-paper reassignments are prohibited. Duplicate extractor use is prohibited. Concurrent conflicting edits cannot both succeed: a multiuser host must use atomic transactions and optimistic lock/expected version checks, with a clear This paper changed—reload/reconcile message. Local file autosaves should use write-to-temp + fsync/atomic rename as appropriate and should keep recoverable backups. Reviewer action timestamps use timezone-aware ISO format and may record authenticated user ID; UTC for storage is recommended.
20.4 Review import/replay safety
An imported review-state JSON must match the gold/extractor input hashes, schema version, mapping and relevant algorithm policy. Reject or require explicit migration on mismatch; never blindly apply E6 from a different uploaded file. Applying replayed events should be deterministic and should return a clear report of invalid/conflicting events. A reviewer can export both a human-readable history and the machine-readable state that recreates effective metrics.

21.1 General testing philosophy
Tests must exercise the standalone installed package and actual entrypoint, not only private helper methods. Never copy old metric totals into tests as fabricated new truth.

22. Implementation phases, migration and version control
22.1 Build in test-gated layers, not one giant Codex rewrite
Phase 1 — Standalone core: typed models, workbook adapters, source mappings, paper linking, significant-only scope, lossless normalization, dedup, global/full-gold DP, candidate graph, strict anchors, name evidence, one-to-one resolver, conditional global tie-breaker and automatic audit. Prove fresh-clone independence. Gate: synthetic paper tests and deterministic engine API pass.
Phase 2 — Scoring and review semantics: implement four value pass flags, both metric populations with attempted-paper rules, immutable auto results, review event overlay and replay/undo/reassign. Gate: all M-series tests and numerical reconciliation pass.
Phase 3 — Streamlit dashboard and exports: upload mappings, compact KPI controls, per-paper details, in-place manual review, green/yellow/red sequence trace, XLSX and JSON. Gate: end-to-end actual user workflow passes without Codex.
Phase 4 — Legacy parity and deployment: run approved frozen historical fixtures; explicitly document policy-induced deviations from rushed evaluator, profile speed, pin package versions, prepare Windows launcher and team README, optionally deploy a restricted lab-hosted instance. Gate: PI can operate app without opening a Python file and developer can reproduce outputs from hashes/config.
Codex may implement these phases, but each phase should be reviewed and tested before the next. Never ask it to re-audit every historical XLSX and PDF as part of a normal end-user Run. Full deep reproducibility checks belong in developer CI/explicit audit mode, not a routine score calculation.
22.2 Legacy code extraction boundary
Reuse tested algorithmic ideas and optionally independently migrated functions from the rushed evaluator: global and full-gold fitting DP, deterministic traceback, unique candidate evidence, Hungarian assignment, review integrity checks. Do not import the old modules or retain old top-level runtime dependency paths. Freeze a small behavior-reference corpus; validate migration parity on unchanged cases. The Lite budget changes for short sequences, significant-only scope and new tie-break/metric policy are deliberate differences to version and test, not unexplained regressions.
22.3 Governance for scientific changes
A scientific change includes altering allowed canonicalization, error budget, strict-lock qualifications, name-evidence requirements, tie-break order, duplicate counting, reviewed-status treatment or FP/FN denominator. Such a change needs a version bump, reason, targeted synthetic test, before/after results on the reference fixture and PI/team acknowledgment before replacing the default. Pure cosmetic UI changes should not change the scientific-policy version.
23. Detailed execution walk-throughs
23.1 Case A: the ideal straightforward strict match
An attempted linked paper has one significant scoreable gold G1=ACGTACGT and extractor E1=acgt acgt with standardizable whitespace and case. Source records and fingerprints are preserved. Canonical strings become identical. Dedup stage finds no duplicate. Unique strict whole lock associates G1↔E1, and records MATCHED_STRICT. Name stage and contextual solver have no remaining nodes to resolve. Both global and semiglobal distances are zero. All four value flags pass. With G=E=M=T=1, all-sequences and matched-only scores are 100%; review not required. The raw extractor still shows its exact original case/spacing in provenance.
23.2 Case B: a full-length extraction of a gold core
A significant 40-nt gold is an exact internal region of an 84-nt extractor. Standardization leaves both strings intact. Strict whole fails, so no S1 lock. Item-local name, if present, is added as evidence. S3 fits every gold base to the contiguous 40-nt extractor region; d_semiglobal=0, strict_core=true. It records prefix and suffix overhangs in gray and fitted gold bases in green. If this is the unique credible association in the paper and no identity conflicts exist, S7 accepts it. strict_whole=false; whole tolerant will likely fail if 44 overhangs exceed the budget. Do not penalize the free flanks for core scoring. Global completion cannot veto a uniquely credible core candidate solely because the strings have different reported scopes.
23.3 Case C: the conflicting 40/84 full-vs-core example
Within one paper, G1 is a 40-nt core, G2 is the corresponding 84-nt full string, E1 is the 84-nt full string and E2 is the exact 40-nt core. Unique strict locks identify G1↔E2 and G2↔E1 if there are no identity collisions; otherwise the graph can achieve the same resolution. A less restrictive early semi-global matcher might see G1↔E1 at 100% and steal E1, but Lite never greedily consumes it. When conditional global alignment is needed, it shows exact whole matches at G1↔E2 and G2↔E1. Record all candidate edges so the audit reveals the discarded G1↔E1 plausible core fit. Both accepted pairs pass whole/core exact and tolerant. No double use and no forced match.
23.4 Case D: named candidate with wrong nucleotides
An extractor item explicitly says DUX4, and the gold has curated name DUX4, but two nucleotide positions differ in a 40-nt string. S2 performs its mini-alignment using the shared engine; name agrees, d=2, budget is two. S3 still compares that gold against every other eligible extractor, and other significant golds against this extractor. If the name-supported pair is uniquely credible and conflict-free, association can be accepted even though it is not strict. Under Strict core it fails, under Adaptive tolerant core it passes, and under whole modes the result depends on the whole distance. If the name instead points to an unrelated sequence outside budget, no name-only TP is awarded; review receives the explicit contradiction.
23.5 Case E: contested near-duplicate golds
G1 and G2 are distinct significant gold variants differing by one nucleotide. E1 differs by one nucleotide from both and is the only extractor. Both pairs are within one-edit tolerance. Gold-near-duplicate and candidate competition are flagged. Name evidence may help if sequence-local and credible, but cannot silently override an actual contradiction. Conditional global tie-break is attempted only if it can distinguish; if the global distances remain equal, the component stops at MANUAL_REVIEW. E1 remains one source item, so neither gold is double-counted as recovered. Closed-world provisional metrics reflect no unconfirmed TP; reviewer sees both candidate alignments.
23.6 Case F: manual reviewer confirms a bad sequence
G1 and E1 are thought to belong to the same aptamer but d_semiglobal=5 for L=40, budget two. Automatic matcher abstains as outside tolerance. In Paper Review, the reviewer selects G1 and E1, checks source/name/provenance, and confirms association. The event establishes MATCHED_HUMAN, not TP. Since d=5>B(40)=2, strict core and tolerant core both fail; matched-only correctness includes this as a wrong sequence, while all-sequences closed-world still counts one FP for E1 and one FN for G1. No nucleotide bases are edited or re-aligned to improve the result.
23.7 Case G: paper complete but extra predictions remain
A paper has three significant golds and seven unique extractor sequences. Three golds are confidently associated, all pass adaptive core; four extractors remain unassigned. The review button is not mandatory because all significant gold identities are resolved and no material conflict remains. All-sequences closed-world counts TP=3, FP=4, FN=0: precision 3/7≈42.86%, recall 100%, F1 6/10=60%. Matched-only correctness is 3/3=100% and significant recovery 3/3=100%. The app displays the caveat that the four extras might include valid non-significant discoveries, and labels their FP status relative to significant reference, not verified hallucination.
23.8 Case H: attempted paper without any extracted sequences
The selected extractor batch includes the paper key, but its aptamer_seq value is an empty list. The gold has two significant scoreable records. Parser correctly treats the paper as attempted-empty, not unattempted. G=2, E=0, M=T=0; significant recovery 0%, closed-world recall 0%, F1 0 under explicit zero-TP convention and precision N/A. Both gold records have no accepted association and may be explicitly marked reviewed missing without changing TP. No dummy extractor values or fabricated FP are created.
24. Decisions intentionally deferred and change-control policy
This is a frozen functional design, but not every conceivable scientific question has a validated quantitative threshold. The following choices remain deliberately conservative/explicit rather than silently guessed:
Exact operational acceptance classifier for tricky short/low-complexity tolerant candidates. The agreed qualitative rule is strong identity and unique support or manual review. The implementation must start conservatively, expose reason codes, and be validated against curated hard fixtures; it must not invent a probability threshold that appears calibrated.
How a global difference becomes reliably decisive in pathological gold-near-duplicate conflicts. No arbitrary completion-margin percentage is authorized. Clear unique preference under documented hierarchy may resolve; equivalent or materially contradictory cases must abstain. Any future numeric margin requires explicit scientific approval and regression tests.
Support for modified aptamer notation, IUPAC wildcard semantics, reverse complement or RNA/DNA translation. Off by default; diagnostic warnings only unless separate policy approved.
How distinct named biological aptamers with identical base strings should contribute to a future raw-item precision definition. v0.1 preserves multiplicity and prevents destructive duplicate suppression; scientific multiplicity cases should abstain rather than be guessed. Default nonconflicting scoring remains unique canonical predictions.
Pool and Kd matching specifics. This master design covers the sequence module completely and its app surface; additional fields require separate scientific contracts before they are scored.
Remote hosting and collaborative persistence provider. The local single-user browser app is the baseline. Institutional security requirements must determine shared hosting/authentication and storage; do not assume a public URL or free cloud tier is authorized for unpublished research workbooks.
Whenever a decision is deferred, the default implementation behavior is audit, flag, and abstain; it must not quietly count a success, delete data, or alter the denominator.
